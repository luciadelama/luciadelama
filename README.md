## Hi there 👋

**Hello, I'm Lucía! Nice to meet you.**
I am Spanish, I grew up in Alcorcón, just south of Madrid, a place that somehow combines the energy of a big city with the familiarity of a neighborhood where everything feels close.

My curiosity about science started with a very simple question: **why was I the only redhead in my family?**

Years later, a biology lesson in high school finally gave me an answer. We were looking at inherited traits across our families, and I discovered how recessive traits could explain something I had wondered about for years. It was a small realization, but it made genetics suddenly feel very real and I wanted to understand more.

That curiosity eventually led me to study Biotechnology at the Technical University of Madrid (UPM), where I specialized in Computational Biotechnology. Somewhere between biology, programming and increasingly large datasets, I discovered bioinformatics and realized I did not really want to choose between biology and data science if I could do a combination of both.

So, two years ago, I packed my things and moved to Copenhagen to pursue an MSc in Bioinformatics at Technical University of Denmark (DTU). It turned out to be one of the best decisions I have made. Professionally, it pushed me far outside what I already knew; personally, it taught me how much I enjoy putting myself in completely new environments and figuring things out along the way.

My first job was not in science at all. During my bachelor's, I worked as a mystery shopper for a marketing company, collecting and structuring information from websites. Looking back, it was probably my first paid data-collection job (just with considerably more Excel and less biology). More importantly, it taught me how to balance university with work and how to operate within a professional team.

My first real contact with research came at the National Center for Biotechnology (CNB-CSIC) in Madrid, where I joined a bioinformatics team supporting different research groups across the institute. The variety was what I enjoyed most: one day I could be working with transcriptomic data, another exploring metagenomics or structural predictions. It was also where many of the tools I had learned at university became real: Linux, HPC environments, R, data cleaning, pipelines and visualizations were no longer exercises, they were part of answering actual biological questions.

Finding my first job in Denmark was not quite as straightforward. My first Danish job was actually in a restaurant, and also my first experience working directly with customers. It had very little to do with bioinformatics, but it gave me something equally important at the time: independence (and the free food and good tips definitely helped). Once I had found my footing in Copenhagen, other opportunities started appearing.

One of those opportunities was a Teaching Assistant for Introduction to Systems Biology at DTU. Only a year earlier I had been one of the students sitting in that classroom, so suddenly being on the other side, explaining concepts, debugging problems and helping students was a surprisingly rewarding experience. 

In Denmark, finding a job was a tough journey but mereció la pena. Todo empezó en un restaurante, mi primera experiencia trabajando frente al publico pero fue super enriquecedor, con comida gratis y buenas propinas. Esto me hizo ser completamente independiente y a partir de ahi, todo fue más facil. Me llegó la oportunidad de trabajar como teacher assistant en DTU, en el curso de Intro to Systems Biology, fue fascinante poder estar en el otro lado de la clase, ayudar a los alumnos que estaban ahi como yo un año antes.

Más tarde, debido a mi interés en genetics, decidí preguntar por un special course en el departamento de medicina genomica en el Rigshopitalet, donde desarollé una shiny app para automatizar un analisis que realizaban diariamente los del laboratorio, usando Docker hoy pueden usarla cada día en el hospital. Tras finalizar el special course, me quede en el departamento como student assistant y me propusieron desarollar una pagina web, con su frontend y su backend, algo que no había hecho antes, fue todo un reto pero aprendí muchas cosas nuevas. 

My interest in genetics eventually brought me to the Department of Genomic Medicine (now, MDxCore) at Rigshospitalet. I initially reached out simply looking for the possibility of doing a special course there. That small step turned into one of my most valuable professional experiences. I developed a Shiny application to automate an analysis that laboratory staff were performing manually as part of their routine. I containerized it with Docker, and today the tool can be used directly by the lab as part of their workflow. After the project, I stayed in the department as a Student Assistant. My next assignment was very different: build a complete web application, frontend and backend included. I had never built one before. So I learned. That experience probably describes quite well how I like to work: I enjoy being given a problem slightly outside what I already know and figuring out what I need to learn to solve it.
